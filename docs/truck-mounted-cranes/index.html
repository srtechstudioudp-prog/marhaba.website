A truck mounted crane is a crane boom fixed on a standard truck chassis. It drives at road speed, sets up quickly on outriggers and suits light to medium lifts and deliveries. Marhaba Heavy Equipment sources truck mounted cranes from Sharjah, UAE and ships them to buyers worldwide.

What is a truck mounted crane and how does it work?

A truck mounted crane uses a commercial or custom truck chassis. The crane boom, slewing mechanism and outriggers are mounted behind the cab or on the rear. A power take-off (PTO) from the truck engine drives the hydraulic pump for the crane.

On site the driver extends the outriggers to level the truck, then operates the boom from controls on the crane or by remote. Telescopic booms are common. Some models have knuckle booms for placing loads in tight spaces.

Key features to look for

  • Boom type (telescopic or knuckle) and length
  • Maximum capacity and capacity at working radius
  • Chassis make, axle configuration and condition
  • Outrigger spread and stability control
  • Safety systems: load moment indicator, overload protection
  • Remote control and winch options

Benefits

  • Fast deployment for short jobs
  • Lower purchase and running cost than larger cranes
  • Carries its own load on the same chassis
  • Easy access to truck parts and servicing

Applications and use cases

  • Building supply: unloading and placing steel, pipes, blocks and precast
  • Utility work: installing poles, pipes and equipment
  • Machinery moves: loading and unloading small machines
  • Workshop and yard use: handling engines and heavy components
  • Construction sites: lifting materials to low levels

How to choose

  1. List the heaviest item and the radius it is lifted at.
  2. Check the load chart, not only the headline capacity.
  3. Match the chassis to the roads and axle load rules in your country.
  4. Inspect the boom, outriggers, hydraulics and truck condition.
  5. Plan for operator training and a service schedule.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by price and underestimating lift radius
  • Not checking the truck chassis as carefully as the crane
  • Ignoring outrigger spread on tight sites
  • Overlooking hydraulic leaks and boom wear

Standards and care

Mobile cranes are expected to meet recognised safety standards and local lifting regulations, with periodic inspection and records. Rules vary by country, so confirm what your authority needs for imported trucks and cranes. These are general notes.

Questions to ask before buying a truck mounted crane

About the crane

Ask for the load chart at your working radius and for the date of the last boom and outrigger inspection. Check whether remote controls and the overload system work.

About the truck

The chassis decides road legality and running cost. Ask for engine hours or mileage, axle loads, brake condition and whether the PTO and hydraulic pump have been serviced.

About shipping and paperwork

A crane truck is a registered vehicle in many countries. Confirm export and de-registration papers, and the width, height and weight for your destination port and roads.
