What is an EOT crane and how does it work?

An EOT crane has one or two bridge girders that span the building, carried on end carriages that run along rails on the building columns or runway beams. A hoist with a trolley travels along the girder, so the hook can reach any point in the rectangle under the crane.

Three motions work together: long travel along the runway, cross travel of the trolley, and hoisting. Operators control them from a pendant, a cabin or a radio remote.

Key features to look for

Capacity, span and duty class

Capacity is the rated load, span is the distance between runway rails, and duty class describes how hard the crane is worked. A crane rated for light occasional use will fail early in a foundry or steel mill.

Hoist and trolley

Check the hoist type (wire rope or chain), speeds, brake condition and the limit switches that stop the hook at the top and bottom of its travel.

Controls and safety

Pendant, cabin and radio remote systems differ. Look for overload limiters, emergency stop and a digital load display if the crane is used near its rated limit.

Benefits of an overhead crane

  • Uses airspace instead of floor space
  • Moves loads safely along a fixed, repeatable path
  • Runs all day at lower cost per lift than a mobile crane
  • Can be upgraded with radio remote control and load display

Applications and use cases

  • Fabrication and machine shops: plate, beams and fixtures
  • Factories and assembly lines: moving components between stations
  • Power stations and plants: lifting motors and pumps for maintenance
  • Precast and storage yards: gantry cranes for slabs and pipes
  • Warehouses: handling heavy stock
  • Workshops for mining and rail: lifting heavy components

Used EOT and jib cranes

Used EOT cranes and used jib cranes are part of our listing. A used crane can save time, but it needs a careful inspection of the girders, welds, end carriages, wheels, rails, hoist, brakes and the electrical panel. Ask for photos of the data plate and of the crane under load if possible, and check that the crane suits the duty you need.

How do I choose an EOT crane?

  1. Measure the span between runway rails and the hook height you need.
  2. Confirm the heaviest load and how often it is lifted (duty class).
  3. Check the building: runway beams must carry the crane's wheel loads.
  4. Choose pendant, cabin or radio control to suit the operators.
  5. Plan installation, commissioning and the inspection your country requires.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing capacity without checking duty class
  • Forgetting that the building must support the crane
  • Ignoring runway rail condition and alignment
  • Skipping the electrical supply details (voltage, frequency, power feed)
  • Buying a used crane without checking the data plate and records

Standards and approvals

Overhead cranes are generally designed and inspected under recognised standards (for example ISO and FEM or EN classifications) and local lifting rules, which usually require periodic examination by a competent person. Requirements differ by country. These are general notes, not claims about a specific crane.

Frequently asked questions

What does EOT stand for?

EOT stands for electric overhead travelling crane. It is a bridge crane that runs on rails near the ceiling of a building.

What is the difference between an EOT crane and a gantry crane?

An EOT crane runs on rails fixed to the building structure. A gantry crane stands on its own legs and runs on the floor, so it works outdoors or where the building cannot carry a runway.

Do you supply used EOT cranes?

Yes, used EOT cranes and used jib cranes are in our listing. Condition is stated in the offer, with pictures and inspection details where available.

Can you supply jib and monorail cranes?

Yes. Jib cranes suit single workstations, and monorail cranes suit fixed routes. Send the capacity and layout.

What information do you need to quote an EOT crane?

Capacity, span, hook height, runway length, duty, power supply, and the delivery port.

What is duty class?

It describes how often and how heavily a crane is used. A higher class means heavier use and a sturdier build. Always match the class to your real duty.

Can radio remote control be added?

Radio remote control is commonly fitted or retrofitted. See EOT crane components.

Do you supply parts for existing cranes?

Yes. Hoists, hooks, limit switches, festoon systems, busbar and more are on EOT crane components.

Does the building need to be strengthened?

Maybe. The runway beams and columns must carry the crane's wheel loads. Have a structural engineer check before you order.
