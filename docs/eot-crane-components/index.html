What does each power feed system do?

Festoon system

A festoon carries flexible cable on small trolleys along a track, so a moving hoist or crane stays connected to power. It is common for lighter duty and short travel.

Busbar and DSL system

Electrical busbar and DSL (down shop lead) systems run a rigid conductor along the runway, with current collectors sliding on it. They suit long travel and heavier duty.

Cable trolleys and reels

Cable trolleys carry the flexible cable, and reels wind it on and off for machines that travel in one direction and back.

How do I identify the right crane part?

  1. Photograph the crane data plate and the part.
  2. Note the crane make, model, capacity and year if known.
  3. For electrical parts, note voltage, frequency and current rating.
  4. Measure the part and its mounting points.
  5. Send everything with your enquiry.

Common mistakes

  • Ordering electrical parts without checking voltage
  • Replacing a hook without checking its rating and markings
  • Fitting a limit switch of a different type or housing
  • Using worn wire rope with a new hoist
  • Ignoring the inspection rules in your country for safety devices

Standards and care

Lifting components and safety devices are usually expected to meet recognised standards and to be inspected by a competent person at set intervals. Hooks and rope in particular should never be modified or repaired casually. These are general notes, not claims about any specific product.

What is a DSL system?

A down shop lead system uses a rigid conductor and sliding collectors to power a moving crane along its runway.

What is the difference between a festoon system and a busbar?

A festoon carries flexible cable on trolleys, while a busbar uses a rigid conductor with collectors. Busbars suit longer travel and heavier duty.
