Crawler cranes in the UAE

A crawler crane is a lifting machine mounted on steel tracks, with a lattice or telescopic boom. The wide tracks spread weight, so it can lift very heavy loads on soft or uneven ground and travel with a load on site. Marhaba Heavy Equipment sources crawler cranes from Sharjah, UAE.

A power project has a 400-ton lift planned for the middle of a muddy site. A wheeled crane would need heavy ground preparation. A crawler crane spreads its weight over its tracks and lifts from where it stands.

What is a crawler crane and how does it work?

A crawler crane has an upper structure with the boom, hoists and counterweight, mounted on a track undercarriage. The tracks give a large footprint, so ground pressure is low and the crane is stable without outriggers.

Most large crawler cranes use a lattice boom made of steel sections that are bolted together on site. The operator uses the load chart to select boom length, radius and counterweight for each lift. Because the crane is slow, it is moved between sites on trailers and assembled again.

Key features to look for

Boom and jib set-ups

Check which boom sections, luffing or fixed jibs and attachments are included. Missing sections are a common cause of delay.

Counterweight and superlift

Capacity depends on counterweight. Confirm what counterweight is supplied and whether a superlift or tray is included.

Undercarriage

Check track pads, rollers and the slew ring. See undercarriage parts for components.

Benefits of crawler cranes

  • Highest stability on soft, uneven ground
  • No outriggers, so a smaller working area
  • Capacity to lift very heavy items at long radius
  • Strong on repeat lifts in the same area

Applications and use cases

  • Power plants: boilers, turbines, transformers
  • Refineries and petrochemical plants: vessels and columns
  • Bridges and viaducts: girders and piers
  • Wind and solar: foundations and heavy components
  • Piling and foundation work: with suitable attachments
  • Ports and heavy yards: large units and modules

How do I choose a crawler crane?

  1. List the heaviest lift, radius and required hook height.
  2. Find the boom and jib set-up on the chart that meets them, with a safety margin.
  3. Check the counterweight, track width and transport weight of each part.
  4. Verify hours, year, service history and condition of the undercarriage.
  5. Confirm that transport to your site and port is feasible, including crane sections.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing capacity at minimum radius only
  • Not checking whether boom sections and counterweight are complete
  • Overlooking ground bearing capacity under the tracks
  • Forgetting transport permits for components
  • Skipping the slew ring and winch inspection

Inspection and care

Ask for pictures and a report on the boom and lattice members, pins and sheaves, hoist winches and rope, slew ring, swing and travel drives, hydraulic systems, track chains and pads, and the load indicator. Regular checks and complete records protect safety and resale value.

Standards and approvals

Crawler cranes are generally expected to meet recognised design and safety standards (for example ISO or EN standards for crane design) and local lifting rules. Importing countries may require inspection certificates. This is general information, not a claim about a specific machine.

Frequently asked questions

What is a crawler crane used for?

Heavy lifts on power, refinery, bridge and industrial projects, especially on soft ground or on sites where the crane stays for weeks.

How much can a crawler crane lift?

Capacity depends on model, boom length, radius and counterweight. Our crane listings span 20 to 1,600 tons across types. Send your lift for matching.

What is the difference between a lattice boom and a telescopic boom?

A lattice boom is assembled from steel sections and is lighter for its length, giving long reach. A telescopic boom extends hydraulically and sets up faster but is typically shorter for the same weight.

How is a crawler crane moved?

It is dismantled into sections and moved on trailers, then assembled again. It can travel short distances on its tracks within a site.
