Wheel loaders in the UAE

A wheel loader is an articulated, wheeled machine with a front bucket, used to load trucks, move stockpiles and carry material over short distances. Marhaba Heavy Equipment sources wheel loaders from Sharjah, UAE for quarries, yards and construction sites and ships them worldwide.

A quarry loads trucks every four minutes. If the bucket is too small, the trucks wait. If it is too large, the loader cannot reach the hopper. The right match between bucket and truck is worth more than raw horsepower.

What is a wheel loader and how does it work?

A wheel loader has a front-mounted bucket on loader arms and a rear engine for balance. The machine is articulated: the front and rear halves pivot, so it steers by bending in the middle.

Hydraulics lift and tilt the bucket. The operator scoops material from a pile, raises the arms and drives to the truck or hopper to dump.

Key features to look for

  • Bucket capacity and breakout force
  • Operating weight and tipping load
  • Engine power and transmission
  • Tyre condition (a significant cost)
  • Axle and articulation joint wear
  • Cab, visibility and attachments

Benefits

  • Quick cycles for loading
  • Mobility around the site on wheels
  • Less surface damage than tracks
  • Wide attachment range

Applications and use cases

  • Quarries: loading blasted rock and aggregate
  • Mines: loading haul trucks and feeding crushers
  • Stockyards and ports: handling bulk materials
  • Construction: loading trucks, moving materials
  • Recycling and waste yards

How to choose

  1. Match bucket volume to the truck size (usually three to five passes).
  2. Check the material density and size.
  3. Select tyre type for the surface.
  4. Inspect articulation joint, axles and tyres.
  5. Plan attachments and parts support.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a bucket that ignores material density
  • Neglecting tyre cost and condition
  • Missing worn articulation pins
  • Underestimating transport size

Inspection and care

Request pictures and a report on the engine, transmission, axles, articulation joint, loader arms and pins, bucket and cutting edge, tyres, cab and safety systems. Daily greasing and tyre pressure checks pay back quickly. See hydraulic parts and engine parts.

Standards and approvals

Loaders are built to recognised safety and emissions standards for their market. Importing countries may restrict used machine age. Check local rules. These notes are general.

Buckets, tyres and attachments

Bucket choice

Rock buckets have strengthened floors and teeth, while light-material buckets hold more volume for coal, wood chips or grain. Choose by material density.

Tyre choice

Tyres can be a large share of running cost. Deeper treads suit rock, while smoother treads suit paved yards and ports.

Forks and grapples

Pallet forks, log grapples and scrap grabs let one loader handle several jobs. Check the coupler type and the hydraulic circuit for each.

Frequently asked questions

What is a wheel loader used for?

Loading trucks and hoppers, moving stockpiles and carrying material over short distances.

How do I choose bucket size?

Match it to the truck so it fills in about three to five passes, and consider material density.

What is the difference between a wheel loader and a backhoe loader?

A wheel loader is a larger machine with only a front bucket. A backhoe loader has a front loader and a rear digging arm in one smaller machine.

Which brands can you source?

Are tyres important?

Yes. Tyres are a major running cost. Ask for tyre condition in the inspection report.
