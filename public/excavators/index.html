What is an excavator and how does it work?

An excavator is a hydraulic machine. The engine drives pumps that push oil to cylinders on the boom, stick and bucket, and to motors that swing the cab and drive the tracks or wheels. The operator controls each movement with joysticks.

The upper structure swings 360 degrees on the undercarriage, so the machine can dig on one side and load a truck on the other without moving.

Key features to look for

  • Operating weight and bucket capacity
  • Engine power and hydraulic flow
  • Boom and stick length for reach and depth
  • Undercarriage condition (tracks, rollers, sprockets)
  • Quick coupler and auxiliary hydraulics for attachments
  • Cab condition, air conditioning and safety features

Benefits

  • One machine for digging, loading and handling
  • Precise control at the bucket
  • Many attachments extend what it can do
  • Parts and service are widely available for common brands

Applications and use cases

  • Foundations and basements: bulk excavation
  • Utilities and pipelines: trenching and backfilling
  • Quarries: loading blasted rock into trucks
  • Demolition: with breakers and shears
  • Roads and drainage: ditching and shaping
  • Mining support: large machines for bulk loading

How do I choose the right excavator?

  1. Define the digging depth and reach you need.
  2. Match the bucket size to the trucks you load.
  3. Check access: width, ground, transport limits.
  4. Verify condition: hours, hydraulics, undercarriage, engine.
  5. Plan attachments and spare parts from the start.

Common mistakes

  • Buying on hours alone and ignoring undercarriage wear
  • Missing a quick coupler or hydraulic line for the attachment you need
  • Not checking pin and bush wear on the boom and stick
  • Ignoring transport width and weight at the destination

Inspection and care

Ask for pictures and a report covering the engine, hydraulic pumps and cylinders, swing bearing, boom and stick welds, bucket and teeth, tracks and rollers, and electronics. Regular greasing, filter changes and track tension checks extend life. See hydraulic parts and undercarriage parts.

Standards and approvals

Excavators are normally built to recognised safety and emissions standards for their market, and many countries restrict the age or emissions level of imported used machines. Check the rules at your destination. These are general notes, not claims about specific machines.

Attachments that change what an excavator can do

Buckets

A general-purpose bucket digs most soils. Rock buckets add reinforcement, and wide ditching buckets shape drains and slopes without scarring the finish.

Hydraulic breakers

A breaker turns an excavator into a demolition and rock tool. It needs the correct auxiliary hydraulic circuit and a carrier heavy enough to carry it.

Couplers and grapples

A quick coupler swaps tools in seconds, and a grapple handles scrap, timber and demolition waste. Confirm the coupler type matches the attachments you already own.

Frequently asked questions

What size excavator do I need?

Start with digging depth, reach and the trucks you load. A medium class machine covers most general construction work, while large machines suit quarries and bulk earth moving.

What is the difference between a crawler and a wheeled excavator?

Crawler machines are stable on soft and rough ground. Wheeled machines drive on roads and suit paved sites and frequent moves.

Which brands can you source?

Customers ask for Caterpillar, Komatsu, Hitachi, Kobelco, Hyundai, Doosan and Volvo. Brand names are for reference only.

How do I check the condition of a used excavator?

Review the inspection report and pictures covering hydraulics, undercarriage, engine, swing bearing and structure.

Can I specify the working hours?

Yes. State the maximum hours and year range in your enquiry.

Do you supply attachments?

Ask us for the attachments you need, such as buckets, breakers or quick couplers, and we will check availability.

How is an excavator shipped?

Usually by sea, on a roll-on/roll-off vessel or in a container for smaller sizes. See export.

Do you supply parts for excavators?

Yes. Hydraulic, engine and undercarriage parts. See spare parts.

What is operating weight?

It is the weight of the machine ready to work, with fluids and operator. It is a common way to describe excavator size.
