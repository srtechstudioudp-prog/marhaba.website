How do I choose the right size?

Size by the work, not by the price. Estimate daily volume, haul distance and the trucks you load, then work back to bucket capacity and machine class. A machine that is too small will run flat out and wear fast, while one that is too large wastes fuel and cannot work in tight spaces.

What should I check on a used machine?

  • Hour meter against condition, not alone
  • Hydraulic leaks, cylinder rods and pump response
  • Undercarriage wear on tracks, rollers and sprockets
  • Engine smoke, oil condition and cold start
  • Bucket, teeth and cutting edge wear
  • Service records and serial number match

Heavy equipment terms explained

Operating weight

The weight of a machine ready to work, including fluids and operator. It is the usual way to describe the size of an excavator, dozer or loader, and it decides transport limits and ground pressure.

Bucket capacity and breakout force

Bucket capacity is the volume it carries, and breakout force is the push the bucket can generate to cut into material. Together they show how well a machine will load your material.

Undercarriage

The tracks, rollers, idlers and sprockets under a crawler machine. It is a major wear cost, so its condition matters as much as the engine hours.

Hour meter

It records engine running time. Useful, but it must be read against real wear on pins, controls and tracks, because use in sand or rock ages a machine faster than the hours suggest.
