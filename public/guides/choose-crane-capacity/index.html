How to choose the right crane for your lift

To choose a crane, find the heaviest load, the distance it must be lifted at (radius) and the hook height, then check a load chart for a crane that lifts that load at that radius with margin. Ground conditions, site access and duration then decide between all terrain, crawler, truck mounted or tower.

A project manager asks for a 100-ton crane because the load is 40 tons. At 30 metres radius that crane lifts 18 tons. The lift fails on paper in ten minutes, if someone opens the load chart. Many failures begin with the headline number.

What do I need to know before choosing a crane?

  1. Load weight: include rigging, hook block and attachments.
  2. Radius: horizontal distance from the crane centre to the load.
  3. Hook height: how high the load must go, plus clearance.
  4. Ground and access: bearing capacity, slope, width, overhead obstacles.
  5. Duration and frequency: one lift, a week of lifts or a multi-month project.

How do I read a crane load chart?

A load chart is a table of maximum loads for each boom length and radius. Find the radius row you need, move to the boom length column that gives your hook height, and read the capacity. That number must exceed your total load with a safety margin set by your lifting plan.

Capacity drops quickly as radius increases. Always read the chart for the exact configuration (counterweight, outrigger spread, jib), not the headline rating.

Multi-site lifts, firm groundAll terrain craneFast set-up and travel
Very heavy lift, soft groundCrawler craneStability and capacity
Light lifts and deliveriesTruck mounted craneQuick and economical
Tall building, repeated liftsTower craneHeight and reach over the site

What about ground conditions?

The crane transfers its load to the ground through outriggers or tracks. If the ground cannot support the pressure, the crane tips or sinks. Use mats to spread load, and check with an engineer on soft or filled ground. Crawler cranes spread load widely, but heavy lifts still need ground checks.

How much safety margin should I allow?

Your lifting plan and local regulations set the margin. As a rule of thumb many planners avoid using the last part of a chart. Do not select a crane that only just meets the load at the radius.

Common mistakes when choosing a crane

  • Using the maximum rating instead of capacity at radius
  • Forgetting rigging and hook block weight
  • Ignoring wind, ground and overhead lines
  • Not checking that all boom sections, jibs and counterweights are included
  • Choosing a crane that cannot be transported to the site

Worked examples (illustrative figures)

A short, heavy lift

A 30-ton load at a 10 metre radius needs a crane whose chart shows more than 30 tons at 10 metres with the boom length you need. A mid-size mobile crane may handle it from a short boom.

A long reach lift

The same 30-ton load at a 30 metre radius needs a much larger crane, because capacity drops as radius grows. This is why the radius must be measured, not guessed.

A repeated lift over weeks

Fifty lifts a day for three months favour a tower or crawler crane over a hired mobile crane, once you count set-up, travel and the cost of waiting.

Checklist before you order a crane

  • Lifting plan with load, radius and height
  • Load chart for the exact configuration
  • Inspection report and pictures
  • Safety systems working: load moment indicator, limit switches
  • Transport and export plan
  • Operator and inspection requirements in your country

Frequently asked questions

How do I know what crane capacity I need?

Work from the heaviest load, its radius and hook height. Then read the load chart for that radius and boom length.

What is crane radius?

It is the horizontal distance from the crane centre of rotation to the load. Capacity falls as radius grows.

Does the load include the hook block?

Yes. Include rigging, hook block and attachments in the load.

When should I use a crawler crane instead of an all terrain crane?

When the lift is very heavy, the ground is soft, or the crane stays on site for weeks. See crawler cranes.

When is a tower crane better?

For tall buildings and projects with repeated lifts over a large area. See tower cranes.

Can I rely on the tonnage in the crane name?

No. The name gives the maximum rating at a short radius. The load chart gives the real capacity for your lift.

What ground checks do I need?

Check bearing capacity, slope and any underground services. Use mats and consult an engineer where ground is soft or filled.

What documents should I request for a crane?

Load chart, manuals, service and inspection records. See inspect used heavy equipment.
