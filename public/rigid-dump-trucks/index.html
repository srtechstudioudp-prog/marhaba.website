Rigid dump trucks and quarry trucks in the UAE

A rigid dump truck is an off-highway haul truck with a fixed frame and a hydraulically raised body, built to carry large loads of rock and ore in mines and quarries. What is a rigid dump truck and how does it work?

A rigid dump truck has a fixed chassis (it does not bend in the middle) and a large body that tilts up on hydraulic cylinders to dump its load. Diesel engines drive the wheels, often through mechanical or electric drive systems on very large trucks.

Rigid trucks are built for mine and quarry haul roads, with heavy axles, large tyres and strong bodies. They differ from articulated dump trucks, which bend in the middle and handle softer, steeper ground.

Key features to look for

Payload and body

Match payload to the loader or excavator so the truck fills in a few passes. Check the body floor, liners and hoist cylinders for wear and cracks.

Engine and drive train

Check engine hours, smoke, oil condition, transmission shifts and axle seals. Parts for Cummins and Komatsu or Caterpillar engines are widely used.

Tyres and brakes

Tyres are a major cost on haul trucks. Ask for tread depth, cuts and age, and for brake and retarder condition.

Applications and use cases

  • Open-pit mines: hauling ore and waste
  • Quarries: moving blasted rock to the crusher
  • Large earthmoving projects: bulk cut and fill
  • Ports and mineral yards: heavy bulk movement
  • Dams and infrastructure: hauling fill material

Benefits of rigid dump trucks

  • High payload and low cost per tonne on good roads
  • Strong, simple structure with long working life
  • Wide parts availability for popular models
  • Match well with excavators and wheel loaders

How do I choose a dump truck?

  1. Choose payload to match the loading machine.
  2. Check haul distance, grade and road condition.
  3. Compare engine, transmission and tyre condition.
  4. Check availability of parts and service.
  5. Confirm transport dimensions and port capacity.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing payload without matching the loader
  • Ignoring tyre age and cost
  • Overlooking frame cracks and worn pins
  • Not checking transport size and weight at the port
