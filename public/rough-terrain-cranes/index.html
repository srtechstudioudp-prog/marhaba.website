What is a rough terrain crane and how does it work?

A rough terrain crane (RT crane) is a telescopic boom crane on a short, stiff carrier with four wheels. The operator drives and lifts from one cab. The machine is built for site use, with high clearance and strong axles, rather than long road journeys.

To lift, the operator sets the outriggers and levels the machine, then follows the load chart. Many models are also rated to pick and carry loads at low speed, within chart limits.

Key features to look for

Boom and capacity

Check the capacity at your working radius, not just the rating. Boom length and jib options decide reach and height.

Axles and tyres

Tyre condition is a major cost on rough ground. Ask about tread depth, damage and the drive and steering modes.

Safety systems

Look for a working load moment indicator, anti-two-block system, outrigger monitoring and level indicators.

Benefits

  • Gets to places road-going cranes struggle to reach
  • Lower cost than larger mobile cranes
  • Simple, single-cab operation
  • Strong choice for repeated lifts around a single site

Applications and use cases

  • Substations and plants: setting equipment on foundations
  • Pipelines and utilities: laying pipe and handling materials
  • Quarries and sites: moving blocks and machine parts
  • Oil and gas sites: maintenance lifts on unpaved ground
  • Construction yards: loading and unloading steel and precast

How do I choose a rough terrain crane?

  1. Define the heaviest lift and its radius.
  2. Check the load chart for the boom length you need.
  3. Confirm ground conditions and how the crane will reach the site.
  4. Inspect tyres, axles, boom, outriggers and the hydraulic system.
  5. Plan transport to the site by trailer if needed.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming it can travel long distances on road
  • Reading capacity at minimum radius only
  • Ignoring tyre wear and cost
  • Overlooking the width and weight limits for transport

Inspection and care

Ask for pictures and a report covering the boom sections and pads, outriggers, hydraulic cylinders and hoses, slew ring, tyres and axles, engine and the safety systems. Keep records of every inspection and repair.

Standards and approvals

Mobile cranes are generally expected to meet recognised safety standards and local lifting rules, with periodic inspection. Requirements differ by country. These are general notes, not claims about a specific machine.

Frequently asked questions

What is a rough terrain crane?

A compact four-wheel mobile crane with a telescopic boom, built to work on uneven, unpaved ground.

What is a four wheel crane?

It is another name used for a compact crane with four wheels. A rough terrain crane is the common example.

How is it different from a truck mounted crane?

A truck mounted crane is a boom on a normal truck and suits roads. A rough terrain crane is purpose-built for site ground. See truck mounted cranes.

Usually it is moved by trailer between sites. Check the model and local rules.
