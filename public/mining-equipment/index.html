How does each machine fit the process?

Drills

Exploration drills collect samples to understand the deposit. Production drills make the holes for explosives. Hole size, depth and rock hardness decide the drill type.

Excavators and trucks

A mining excavator loads the truck. The two must be matched so the truck fills in a few passes and neither waits for the other.

Crushers and plants

Crushers reduce rock to a size the next stage can handle. Crushing plants combine feeders, crushers and screens to make graded product.

Benefits of a properly matched fleet

  • Lower cost per tonne
  • Less waiting between loading and hauling
  • Fewer unplanned stops on key machines
  • Easier parts planning when brands are limited

Applications

  • Open-pit and surface mines: drilling, loading and hauling
  • Quarries: aggregate and cement raw material
  • Exploration projects: sampling programmes
  • Civil works: rock excavation and foundation work with piling rigs
  • Material processing yards: crushing and screening

How do I choose mining equipment?

  1. Define the production target and rock type.
  2. Find the bottleneck: drilling, loading, hauling or crushing.
  3. Match machine sizes across the chain.
  4. Check parts support and service for the brand.
  5. Check shipping size and port capacity.

Common mistakes

  • Buying one big machine that the rest of the chain cannot support
  • Ignoring parts support at the site
  • Not checking wear in drills and crusher liners
  • Overlooking transport size and port limits
