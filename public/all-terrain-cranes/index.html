What is an all terrain crane and how does it work?

An all terrain crane (often written AT crane) combines a road-legal carrier with a heavy-duty crane on the same chassis. The carrier has several axles, usually with all-wheel or crab steering, so it can handle narrow sites and soft ground. The crane section has a telescopic boom, hydraulic outriggers and a counterweight.

At the site the operator levels the machine on outriggers, extends the boom to the length the lift needs and uses the load chart to confirm the load is within limits at that radius. Many cranes can lift while a load is moved short distances on tyres, subject to the manufacturer's chart.

Key features to look for

Boom and jib

A telescopic boom gives quick reach. Many models add a lattice jib or extension for more height. Check the boom configuration against the lift you plan.

Carrier and axles

More axles spread weight and allow higher capacity, but need more room to turn. Check axle load limits for road transport in your country.

Safety systems

Look for a load moment indicator, anti-two-block system, outrigger monitoring and slew limiters. These are standard on modern machines and affect resale and import approvals.

Benefits of using an all terrain crane

  • Less time on site because set-up is quick
  • Lower transport cost than a crane that needs a lowbed trailer
  • Safer work on soft or uneven ground thanks to wide outrigger spread
  • Flexible use across many job types without changing the crane

Applications and use cases

  • Plant maintenance: replacing pumps, motors, exchangers during shutdowns
  • Steel and precast erection: setting beams, columns and panels
  • Industrial and port yards: moving containers, machinery and modules
  • Power and renewables: erecting transformers, small turbines, solar equipment
  • Machinery relocation: loading heavy equipment onto trailers
  • Bridge and road work: setting girders on short spans

How do I choose an all terrain crane?

  1. Define the heaviest lift, its radius and the hook height.
  2. Open the load chart and confirm capacity at that radius with the boom length you need.
  3. Check ground bearing and outrigger spread against the site.
  4. Check axle loads, width and length for transport on your roads.
  5. Compare hours, year, service history and parts support for the brand and model.

Common mistakes buyers make

  • Buying by the headline tonnage and not the capacity at working radius
  • Ignoring axle loads and road permits at the destination
  • Skipping boom, slew ring and hoist inspections
  • Forgetting the cost of counterweights and extra jib sections
  • Assuming every safety system is present on an older model

Inspection and care

Before you order, ask for pictures and an inspection report covering the boom sections and pads, slew ring, outriggers, hoist and wire rope, hydraulics, brakes and the load moment indicator. In service, follow the manufacturer's inspection schedule and keep records of every lift and repair.

Standards and approvals

Cranes are normally expected to meet recognised safety standards, such as EN 13000 or ISO standards for mobile cranes, and local lifting regulations. Requirements differ by country, so confirm what your authority needs for imported cranes. These are general notes, not a statement about any specific machine.

Frequently asked questions

What is an all terrain crane used for?

It is used for plant maintenance, steel and precast erection, industrial yards and heavy equipment relocation. Its fast set-up and ability to travel on roads make it suitable for work at several sites.

How is an all terrain crane different from a truck mounted crane?

An all terrain crane has a purpose-built multi-axle carrier with all-wheel steering and a larger capacity range. A truck mounted crane places a boom on a standard truck chassis. See truck mounted cranes.

What capacity range can I get?

Our crane listings range from 20 to 1,600 tons across all types. Availability depends on model and location, so send your lift details.

Which brands are common?

Liebherr, Tadano, Kato, Terex and Demag are commonly requested. Brand names are for reference, and availability varies.

Can I get the load chart before buying?

Yes. Ask for the load chart for the exact machine and boom configuration, and compare it with your radius.

How do I check the condition of a crane?

Review the inspection report and pictures covering boom, outriggers, slew ring, hoist, hydraulics and safety systems. Tell us which items matter most.

Does the price include counterweights and jib?

Not always. State what you need in your enquiry so the offer lists counterweights, jib and extensions clearly.

How is an all terrain crane shipped overseas?

Usually by sea, roll-on/roll-off or break bulk, sometimes with parts removed for size. See export for the process.

Can I get spare parts for the crane later?

Yes. We handle hydraulic parts and other components. Send the model and serial number.
