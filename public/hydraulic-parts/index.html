What do hydraulic parts do?

A hydraulic system turns engine power into movement. A pump pushes oil through valves to cylinders and motors, which move the arms, bucket, swing and travel. Hoses carry the oil, and seals keep it inside.

Because pressure is high, small leaks and contamination cause quick damage. Replacing the right part at the right time prevents larger failures.

How do I identify the right part?

  1. Read the part number stamped on the old part.
  2. Note the machine brand, model and serial number.
  3. Take photos from several angles, including ports and mounting points.
  4. Measure bore, rod diameter and stroke for cylinders.
  5. Send all details with your enquiry.

Common mistakes

  • Replacing a pump without cleaning the system and filters
  • Ordering a seal kit without confirming the cylinder size
  • Ignoring contamination in the oil
  • Mixing hose pressure ratings

Care and standards

Change hydraulic oil and filters on schedule, keep the oil clean, and fit parts to the manufacturer's torque and pressure specifications. Hose assemblies should match the required pressure rating. These are general notes, not claims about specific products.

Reading the signs of hydraulic trouble

Heat

An overheating system points to a worn pump, a blocked cooler or low oil. Heat shortens seal life across the whole circuit.

Noise

Whining often means air or cavitation at the pump inlet, and knocking can point to worn components. Stop before the damage spreads.

Slow or weak movement

Internal leakage in a cylinder or valve gives slow, weak or drifting movement. Test before ordering a replacement to find the real cause.
