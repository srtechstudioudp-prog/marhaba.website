How do I identify the right part?

The part number is the most reliable key, followed by the machine model and serial number. If the old part is damaged or unreadable, send photos from several angles, its dimensions and any numbers stamped on it.

Never order from a description alone. Many parts look identical but differ in thread, pressure rating or spline count.

What affects parts lead time?

  • Whether the part is in stock or must be sourced
  • The brand and age of the machine
  • Weight and size, which decide air or sea freight
  • Your destination and customs requirements

Should I replace parts together?

Yes, when wear is linked. Replacing only a worn track chain on a machine with worn sprockets shortens the life of the new chain. Replacing a pump without checking contamination in the system can destroy the new pump. Ask us about matching sets.

Parts terms explained

OEM, aftermarket and used

OEM parts come from the machine manufacturer or its supplier. Aftermarket parts are made by other firms to fit, and used parts are removed from another machine. Each has a different price, life and risk, so say which you want.

Part number and serial number

The part number identifies the item, and the serial number identifies the exact machine, which can change the correct part within the same model. Send both.

Wear parts and consumables

Wear parts such as cutting edges, track rollers and bucket teeth are replaced on a schedule. Consumables such as filters and seals are replaced more often. Stocking a first set avoids early downtime.

Rebuild kits

A kit bundles seals, gaskets or small parts to rebuild a pump, cylinder or valve. It only fits the exact component version, so confirm the model before ordering.
