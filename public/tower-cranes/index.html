Tower cranes in the UAE

A tower crane is a tall fixed crane with a vertical mast and a horizontal jib, used to lift building materials across a construction site. It offers great height and reach for repeated lifts. Marhaba Heavy Equipment sources tower cranes from Sharjah, UAE for projects at home and abroad.

A tower on a high-rise site makes hundreds of lifts a day. One crane serves every floor, so it must match the reach, the height and the lift weight at the jib tip for the whole project, not only on day one.

What is a tower crane and how does it work?

A tower crane is erected on a base or foundation. A vertical mast supports a rotating jib and a counter-jib with counterweights. A trolley runs along the jib to position the hook, and a hoist raises the load. The slewing unit turns the jib through a full circle.

As the building rises, the crane climbs by adding mast sections, either inside the structure or externally with ties to the building. An operator works from a cab at the top or by remote control.

Key features to look for

  • Maximum capacity and capacity at jib tip
  • Jib length and under-hook height
  • Mast section type and condition
  • Slewing and hoist drives
  • Safety devices and limiters
  • Complete set of ballast and base parts

Benefits

  • Reaches the full work area from one place
  • Frees the ground from mobile crane traffic
  • Efficient for repeated material lifts
  • Scales with the height of the building

Applications and use cases

  • High-rise buildings: lifting concrete, rebar, formwork
  • Residential developments: serving several blocks
  • Infrastructure: bridge pylons, stations
  • Industrial plants: construction of large structures
  • Shipyards and ports: specialised tower arrangements

How to choose a tower crane

  1. Map the building and the area the jib must cover.
  2. Find the heaviest lift and its distance from the mast.
  3. Determine the height needed and how the crane will climb.
  4. Choose hammerhead or luffing jib based on the site.
  5. Check the completeness of mast, jib, ballast and base parts.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing by maximum capacity and not the tip capacity
  • Missing parts such as base sections or ballast
  • Ignoring wind conditions and slewing limits
  • Not planning for erection and dismantling equipment
  • Skipping documentation on past use and inspection

Standards and care

Tower cranes are designed and operated under recognised crane standards and local rules, with erection, inspection and operator qualification requirements. Requirements vary by country. These notes are general and not a claim about any specific machine.

Planning a tower crane project

Foundation and base

The crane needs a foundation designed for its loads and your soil. Ask which base, anchors or ballast come with the offer, because missing base parts delay the start more than any other item.

Erection and climbing

Plan the mobile crane for erection, the climbing frame and the tie-in points to the structure. Self-erecting models shorten this stage on small sites.

Dismantling and shipping

Mast sections, jib parts and counterweights ship separately. Check that the parts list and the pin and bolt sets are complete before the cargo leaves.
